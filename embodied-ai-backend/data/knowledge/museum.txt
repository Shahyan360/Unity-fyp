The Museum of Human Ideas is a teaching gallery of artifact replicas and interpretations, not a collection of originals. Labels distinguish well-supported evidence from uncertain reconstruction.
The Rosetta Stone is a decree from 196 BCE in Egyptian hieroglyphic, Demotic, and Ancient Greek. The Greek text helped scholars decipher hieroglyphs; the stone was not a bilingual dictionary.
The Antikythera Mechanism is a fragmented ancient Greek geared device that modelled astronomical cycles. Its exact original use and display are still debated.
Roman coins carried portraits, titles, and political messages through everyday exchange. Their imagery is evidence of public messaging, not a neutral picture of Roman life.
The Terracotta Army was made for the mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang in third-century BCE China. Its life-sized figures vary in appearance and originally carried real weapons.
Greek amphorae stored goods such as wine and oil. Painted scenes can illuminate myth and social ideals, but archaeologists interpret them alongside their find context.
The Sutton Hoo helmet was buried in an early seventh-century ship burial in England. Its iron, bronze, and garnet decoration shows elite craftsmanship and long-distance connections; the occupant's identity is uncertain.
The Venus de Milo is a Hellenistic Greek marble sculpture found on Milos in 1820 and commonly identified as Aphrodite. Its missing arms leave its original pose and attributes uncertain.
Gutenberg's mid-fifteenth-century European movable-type printing system made books faster and cheaper to reproduce. Printing technologies existed earlier in East Asia.
