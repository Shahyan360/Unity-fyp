An atom has a tiny, dense nucleus made of protons and neutrons. Electrons are described by quantum orbitals—regions of probability—not as little planets on fixed circular paths.
The neutral lithium-7 isotope has three protons, four neutrons, and three electrons; its simplified electron arrangement is two in the first shell and one in the next.
In the Solar System, the Sun contains more than 99 percent of the system's mass. Eight planets orbit it; Earth is third from the Sun. Classroom model distances and sizes are not to scale.
A plant cell has a cell wall for support, a selectively permeable membrane, chloroplasts that capture light energy for photosynthesis, and a nucleus containing most of the cell's DNA.
Roots absorb water and minerals, stems transport materials and provide support, and leaves usually carry out most photosynthesis.
Effective teaching connects a visible model to one concept at a time, then checks the learner's understanding with a short question.
The classroom can show the Solar System and the individual planets Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune; an atom; the periodic table; water molecule; plant and animal cells; DNA; human heart; animal anatomy; sunflower; oak tree; plant parts; water cycle; electric circuit; volcano; light spectrum; and the Pythagorean theorem. If asked what is available, offer this catalog and invite the learner to choose one model.
