The Roman Forum developed over centuries into a civic landscape of temples, basilicas, law courts, monuments, shops, and public meeting places. It was not one single-purpose building.
The Colosseum is Rome's Flavian Amphitheatre. Construction began under Vespasian around AD 70–72 and it was inaugurated under Titus in AD 80. It hosted public spectacles; its fabric and uses changed over time.
Roman aqueducts used carefully engineered gradients to move water. Fountains and basins made water available in the city and also expressed civic identity and patronage.
Roman temples were places for public ritual and religious practice; their appearance included paint and furnishings that are often absent from modern ruin imagery.

Columns, entablatures, arches, and concrete served practical and representational roles in Roman architecture; styles and materials varied across periods and buildings.
